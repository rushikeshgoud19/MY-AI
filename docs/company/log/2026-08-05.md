## Found

**1. Clustly has almost no demand, and says so on its own homepage.**
The banner reads `147 services live · $1,950 settled on-chain`. I verified this by
fetching the raw HTML and grepping for the string rather than trusting a page
summary. $1,950 lifetime across 147 listings is $13.27 average lifetime revenue per
listing. The platform came out of the Colosseum Frontier hackathon (6 Apr – 11 May
2026, winners announced 26 June), so that is roughly four months of trading. Real
escrow program, real engineering, no buyers yet.

**2. The assumption in the map about crypto tax was wrong, and the real problem is
worse.**
The map worried that India's flat 30% VDA tax would gut take-home. That is not how
it works: USDC received for services is business income at INR fair market value on
receipt, taxed at slab rates, and the 30% applies only to the *gain* on later
conversion — which for a stablecoin converted promptly is near zero. So the tax
worry was overstated.

The actual blocker is foreign exchange law, which the map did not consider at all.
Export of services from India must be paid in convertible foreign currency through
an authorised dealer bank. Crypto is not currency under FEMA, generates no
FIRC/e-FIRA, and SolvLegal puts the exposure under FEMA s.13 at up to 3x the
transaction amount plus ED action and possible account freezing. Three independent
sources agree; none dissent.

**Confidence, stated plainly:** those are law-firm and fintech blogs, not RBI
circulars or FEMA notifications read in the original, and not a CA's opinion. I did
not verify against primary law. I am calling it anyway because the asymmetry is
brutal — a 3x penalty against a first sale worth maybe ₹10,000 on a marketplace
that has settled $1,950 total. If you want it overturned, the thing to buy is a
CA's written opinion on FEMA s.7/s.8, not more of my searching.

**3. What I could not verify.** The `clustly-escrow` Solana program ID is not
published in their docs, so I could not check the $1,950 independently on-chain.
That is exactly the audit we sell and I could not run it here. The docs also do not
say whether the counter is lifetime or a rolling window; a rolling window would make
the picture worse, not better, so it does not change the call.

## Addendum, same session — "i have fampay bro"

Rushi answered the payment question with FamPay. Checked it against the operator's
own Terms of Use rather than reasoning about it. **It cannot be the rail.** It is a
prepaid payment instrument issued by Triotech under the RBI PPI Master Directions —
a wallet with no account number or IFSC, so there is nothing to put on an invoice.
Clause 22.2 is explicit: *"No commercial usage: You shall use the Services only for
your lawful and personal use."* And the Small PPI variant cannot transfer funds out
to a bank at all. Full detail and limits recorded in B1; **B1 stays OPEN.**

**Near-miss worth recording.** A search result pointed me at `famapp.com/terms-of-service`
and it returned a clean "personal, non-commercial use" clause that fit the story
perfectly. It is a different company — a US messaging app, nothing to do with
FamPay. I nearly cited it. Caught it because the same page described the product as
video calls and messaging. The correct source is the Triotech PDF, which I then
downloaded and extracted rather than reading a summary of. Recording it because
"the search result confirmed what I expected" is precisely the failure this company
exists to sell against.

**The bigger thing this surfaced:** the FamPay terms treat under-18s as Minors who
cannot accept terms themselves, which prompted the age question. Under s.11 of the
Indian Contract Act 1872 only 18+ are competent to contract, and an agreement with
a minor is void *ab initio* (*Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*, 1903). If Rushi is
under 18 he cannot form a binding service contract in his own name and B1's answer,
and the offer, change shape. Now the top blocker.

## Second addendum — B1 resolved

Rushi came back: **19, PAN yes, savings account no ("waiting for the right time").**
That clears the s.11 contract-capacity worry entirely and unblocks B1, so I took it
rather than waiting for a fresh session over ceremony. Two tickets closed today.

**Answer: he can be paid, domestically, and the rail is deliberately boring —
UPI/IMPS into a savings account in his own name against an individual invoice
carrying his PAN.** Zero fee, zero onboarding, instant settlement. No business
registration (sole proprietor exists by default), no GST (threshold is ₹20 lakh for
services; Telangana is not special-category), no payment gateway.

**The finding that settled domestic-vs-international, and it is not the one I
expected.** I went in thinking the argument would be about fees and FEMA paperwork.
Those are real — Skydo/Wise/Payoneer run 1.9–3% plus FIRA and purpose codes and a
15-month realisation window. But the actual decider is simpler and I nearly buried
it under a comparison table: **every international rail still requires the savings
account he does not have.** International is domestic plus fees plus compliance. It
was never a fork; it was a superset. Domestic-first is forced, not preferred.

Also found: corporate Indian clients withhold **10% TDS under s.194J** above ₹30,000
per contract. Not a loss — it is credited at ITR — but it means a ₹10,000 invoice to
a company pays ₹9,000 now. That is a pricing fact, pushed into B3.

Ruled out for now: payment gateways. Razorpay does onboard unregistered freelancers
(Stripe India does not), but for one customer paying one invoice it adds ~2% and an
onboarding step to solve a problem UPI already solves free.

**Not verified, stated plainly:** I did not read the GST Act, the RBI KYC Master
Direction or s.194J in the original — all of it comes from tax and fintech
write-ups that agree with each other. Four independent sources concur on the ₹20
lakh services threshold. The savings-vs-current-account point is the weakest link:
that savings accounts are "for personal use only" is bank T&C and blog consensus,
not primary law I checked. It does not matter below ~₹50,000/month. And none of it
is *tested* — no account exists, no invoice has been issued, no rupee has moved.
