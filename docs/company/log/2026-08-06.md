## Third session — went looking for buyers, found the offer's biggest hole

Rushi said go again without supplying a recipient name, so I tried to unblock Route C
without one: find people **publicly asking** how to verify an overseas supplier. A
handful of self-identified people with a stated problem is not a scraped list.

**That failed.** The searches returned supplier-vetting SEO content and old Quora
threads — no identifiable person with a live problem. No prospects found.

**But it turned up the strongest objection to our own offer, and I then confirmed it
against our own sample.** The headline finding in the UK sample — "Active, but a
proposal to strike off, accounts overdue five years" — **is printed on the free
Companies House page in plain sight.** Verified in the raw HTML: `Warning` appears
twice, `overdue` four times, `strike off` once, and a visitor reads
`Company status Active - Active proposal to strike off ... Warning Accounts overdue`.

**We were not revealing hidden data. We were reading a public page aloud.** Thirty
seconds, free, no expertise needed. Against B2's own price evidence — checking sold
as checking clears **$5** — the UK version is a $5 service priced at $60. Australia
is the same: ABN Lookup is free and equally legible.

**India is where the friction actually is**, which is where this started before the
widening. MCA returns 403; the usable data sits on obscure paywalled mirrors a
foreign buyer has no reason to know; nothing prints "warning" for you, so the
stale-filings finding has to be **derived**; and those mirrors' own arithmetic was
wrong on 3 of 3 companies tested, so an unaided buyer is actively misled. A foreign
buyer genuinely cannot self-serve an Indian check. They can trivially self-serve a
UK one.

**So the expansion Rushi asked for on 2026-08-05 pointed at the weakest part of the
offer.** Proposed correction: **the pitch re-narrows to Indian companies for foreign
buyers; UK and Australia stay supported but unpromoted** — the script already handles
them and they cost nothing to keep. Capability is not dropped, positioning is.

**Proposed, not applied** — it reverses his instruction, and the B6 listing copy
currently leads with all three jurisdictions.

Worth saying plainly: this is the most useful thing produced today, and it is
entirely negative. It stops us selling the wrong version of the offer.

## Fourth session — Route C died, demand got validated, and I was overruled

**Route C is dead.** It needed one warm contact. Rushi: *"idk"*. That is a real
answer and it closes the route rather than leaving it hanging.

**Went looking for buyer communities and found vendors instead** — Tetra Inspection,
Panoramic Sourcing, Small World India, Globalising, Netyex. An established industry
already sells Indian supplier verification.

**That produced the most decisive number of the week.**
[Tetra publishes its rates](https://tetrainspection.com/supplier-verification-audit/):
**supplier verification from $240 per man-day**, factory audits from $440, standard
engagement 1–3 days desk research **plus a 0.5–1 day on-site visit**.

Two things follow and they cut opposite ways:

- **Demand is validated at a real price.** First hard evidence all week that anyone
  pays for this, from a company confident enough to publish a rate card. Not $5 —
  $240 to $1,000+. B2 could never establish this with Fiverr and Upwork blocked.
- **What they sell is not what we have.** Their product includes a human walking into
  the building. We are the desk-research slice.

**So the offer is re-framed, and it is better for it:** not "supplier verification",
but **"the cheap desk check you run before deciding whether a supplier is worth a
$500 on-site audit."** Triage. It is honest about having no site visit, it explains
the price gap instead of hiding it ($50 vs $240+ is a different *step*, not a
discount), and the "could not verify" section becomes the handoff — precisely the
list of things a site visit would settle.

**And I was overruled, correctly.** I proposed re-narrowing to India on the grounds
that the UK finding sits on the free Companies House page. Rushi: *"i told u look out
of india"* — twice now. **Dropped, not re-litigated.**

The reconciliation is better than either position alone: **the value was never
"hidden data".** Tetra charges $240/man-day for desk research on public records; the
records being public was never the point. And **multi-jurisdiction is what makes
triage work** — a buyer sourcing internationally does not want to learn Companies
House, then ABN Lookup, then CIN formats and which Indian mirror to trust. One
service, one format, whichever country the supplier is in. A single-jurisdiction
service is worth *less* to that buyer.

**Risk carried, stated once, not argued:** a UK or Australian buyer may realise the
register is free and not convert. If UK/AU orders never materialise while Indian ones
do, that is the signal — and it will be evidence rather than argument.

**PeoplePerHour: dead.** Its Due Diligence job board returned **3 results, and none

**Contra: dead.** 0% commission, but "no feed of active job posts... passive inbound"
and reviewers reporting "too few opportunities". Zero percent of nothing. Precisely
the Clustly failure mode, and a good illustration that a low fee is worthless
without buyers.

**Guru: unverified.** Seller-side skill pages exist; no buyer-posted work found.

**Upwork: the only real alternative.** A genuine due-diligence job board with a live
"Due Diligence Analyst" posting, budgets from $75 up, ~10% fee — half of Fiverr — and
an **apply-to-jobs** model that suits a zero-review seller far better than waiting to
be discovered.

**Then the old wall.** Upwork has abolished the free sign-up Connects because bot
farms harvested them. A new freelancer must **buy 100 Connects for $15** or subscribe
at **$19.99/month**, and buying **requires a billing method on file**. Proposals cost
10–24 Connects; the free tier is 10 a month, about **one proposal**.

**Rushi has no card.** This is the identical constraint that killed every cloud free
tier. It has now killed the best marketplace alternative too.

**So the survey's real finding is not which platform — it is that the no-card
constraint is the binding constraint on the whole business.** Every remaining fiat
marketplace either demands a card up front or has no buyers. Which turns the
outstanding bank-account action from abstract prudence into the actual route:

> bank account -> debit card -> Upwork -> a job board with real demand at ~10%

Also recorded: ~30% of new Upwork applications were rejected in 2025, ID verification
is mandatory, and mismatched names auto-reject — which sits badly with the "Shadow"
pseudonym plan.

**Unverified and worth confirming before relying on it:** whether an Indian *debit*
card is accepted for Connects. Upwork's own community carries a "Can't Buy Connects -
From India" thread.
