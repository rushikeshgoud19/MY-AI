# The outreach motion Rushi will actually run

`wayfinder:grilling` (HITL) · **RESOLVED 2026-08-05 — no outreach; a listing** · copy ready to paste

## Question

Claude drafts, Rushi sends. That is fixed and not up for redesign. What is
undecided is the motion around it — and it has to be one Rushi is still running in
three weeks, not one that looks good on day one.

- **Which channel?** Direct email, a marketplace listing where buyers come to you,
  posting build logs publicly, or existing communities. A marketplace needs no
  outreach at all and takes a cut — that trade is a real option here, not a
  cop-out, especially for someone with limited hours.
- **Volume per day Rushi can sustain.** Ten researched messages beat a hundred
  generic ones, and a hundred needs infrastructure and risks the sending domain.
- **What Claude prepares:** per-prospect research, a drafted message, and the
  specific reason this prospect has this problem. Rushi reviews and sends.
- **How a reply is handled.** Claude does not talk to a customer directly — it
  drafts, Rushi sends. Decide the turnaround, because a warm lead goes cold while
  waiting for a human to be awake.

## Hard constraints

No scraped lists, no fake identities, no volume that constitutes spam, no pretending
a message was written by a person who did not read it. Beyond the ethics: it burns
the sending domain and the accounts, and there is no recovering a young business
from that reputation. If the numbers only work at spam volume, the offer is wrong —
go back to the offer.

## Resolution — 2026-08-05: the motion is a listing, and there is no outreach

### Channel: Fiverr

Chosen because it is the only place where demand for this category was actually
observed (B2), and because it requires no outreach at all — which the constraints
above make expensive and the hard rules make slow, since every message needs Rushi's
approval before it goes.

**The economics, honestly.** Fiverr charges a **flat 20% commission** on everything
— base price, extras and tips, no tiers, no exceptions
([FreelanceCompare](https://freelancecompare.com/blog/fiverr-fees-explained),
[HireEcomExperts](https://hireecomexperts.com/fiverr-seller-fees-2026/)). Withdrawal
to India is via Payoneer or bank transfer, with FX taking a further cut
([Karbon](https://www.karboncard.com/blog/how-to-get-paid-on-fiverr-in-india)).

On an $80 order: Fiverr takes $16, leaving $64, and FX leaves roughly $60 —
**about ₹5,000**, order to bank.

**Twenty percent looks bad next to Clustly's four.** It is not. We ruled Clustly out
because it had settled $1,950 in its lifetime. **20% of a marketplace with buyers
beats 4% of one without**, and that comparison is the whole reason this channel wins.

### The narrowing: Indian companies, checked for foreign buyers

Not "due diligence". Specifically:

> **Verify an Indian company from its MCA filing record — and be told what could not
> be confirmed.**

- **Buyer:** a business outside India about to pay an Indian supplier, contractor or
  agency for the first time.
- **Why this is defensible:** the subject matter is Indian registry data, where being
  in India is a genuine edge, and the buyer is international, where the money
  demonstrably is. This resolves the domestic-vs-international tension B2 raised
  without choosing a side — **the buyer is international, the subject is Indian.**
- **Why it is narrow enough:** competing on "due diligence" against sellers with 400+
  reviews is unwinnable. Competing on "Indian company verification" is a category
  small enough to be found in and specific enough to be believed.

### Scope boundaries, written into the listing rather than left implicit

- **Companies only. Never private individuals.** This is where the privacy risk lives
  and it is excluded by design, not by discretion.
- **Not a credit opinion, compliance determination, KYC certification, or advice on
  whether to transact.**
- No external contact details in the gig — Fiverr prohibits it
  ([Fiverr Help](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/37333144004881-Community-Standards-Illegal-and-prohibited-services)).

**Policy check, with its limit stated:** no explicit prohibition on company due
diligence was found, and such gigs are live on the platform today (B2 observed five,
$10–$120). But the prohibited-services list changes and enforcement tightens.
**Rushi should read the current official list before publishing** — this was checked
via search summaries, not by reading Fiverr's live policy page, which is behind the
same block as the rest of the site.

### The risk that could sink this, stated plainly

**A new seller with zero reviews, against sellers with 400–500.** This is the largest
single threat to the plan and no listing copy solves it. The honest position:

- The two sample reports are the only asset a zero-review seller has that an
  established competitor does not — they are *specific* and *checkable*, and both
  contain findings a confident competitor would have flattened.
- **Do not price at the bottom to buy reviews.** It trains the wrong buyer and the
  observed floor for "checking sold as checking" is $5 (B2). Start at the lower end
  of the *serious* band and hold.
- Expect the first order to take a while, or not to come. **If no order arrives, the
  listing is the experiment that failed, not the offer** — and the next move is the
  first real buyer conversation, which is B2's unfinished business anyway.

listings at $80 and $120 (B2). Standard at $60 nets roughly **₹3,700** after Fiverr
and FX. **These are listed prices from competitors, not verified completed-order
values** — the sources that would confirm the latter are blocked.

### How a reply is handled

Fiverr messages arrive from real buyers, so the hard rule applies without exception:
**Claude drafts, Rushi sends.** Proposed protocol:

1. Buyer message arrives → Rushi pastes it in.
2. Claude drafts the reply and, where the buyer names a company, runs the check.
3. Rushi reads and sends. **Nothing goes out that he has not read.**
4. Target turnaround under 12 hours; Fiverr surfaces response time to buyers.

Claude never has Fiverr credentials and never messages a buyer.

### What only Rushi can do

1. **Create the Fiverr seller account.** His identity, his terms acceptance.
2. **Read the current prohibited-services list** before publishing.
3. **Paste the listing copy** (drafted below) and publish it.
4. **Set up payout** — Payoneer or bank transfer, both of which need the bank account

5. **Press send on every buyer message.**

## Listing copy — draft, for Rushi to review and paste

Nothing below has been published. It is a draft awaiting his approval.

**Title**

> I will verify an Indian company from its official filing record

**Description**

> Before you pay an Indian supplier, contractor or agency you have not met, you
> probably want to know they exist.
>
> I check a named Indian company against its public filing record and send you a
> short report where **every single line carries the source it came from** — and a
> section that says plainly what I could **not** confirm.
>
> That last part is the point. Most reports you can buy will hand you a confident
> summary. Mine tells you where the record goes quiet, because that is usually the
> part that matters.
>
> **What you get**
> - Registered status — active, struck off, dormant, under liquidation
> - CIN, incorporation date, registrar, company class
> - Registered address and registered activity code
> - Authorised and paid-up capital
> - Number of directors on record
> - Every figure recomputed, not copied
> - **A "could not verify" section listing exactly what the record does not show**
>
> **What this is not**
> This is not a credit opinion, a KYC or compliance determination, or advice on
> whether to do business with anyone. It reports what the public register says and
> what it does not. **I check companies, never private individuals.**
>
> **Why the "could not verify" section exists**
> While building this I checked three companies against one of the main data
> providers. Its displayed "company age" was wrong on all three — one of them
> showing a negative number of months, the other two showing plausible figures that
> were simply untrue. Numbers that look right and are not are the reason I recompute
> everything rather than repeat it.
>
> Send me the company name, or the CIN if you have it.

**FAQ**

> **Can you check a person rather than a company?**
> No. Companies only. Checks on private individuals are outside what I do.
>
> **Is this an official or certified report?**
> No. It is research on public filing records, with every source shown so you can
> check it yourself. It is not certified by any authority and is not a compliance
> determination.
>
> **How current is the data?**
> The filing mirrors are refreshed periodically, not live, and I state the date
> stamp on every source I use so you can see exactly how fresh each fact is.
>
> **What if the company turns out to be fine?**
> Then you get a report saying so, with the same sourcing, and any open questions
> worth raising. A clean result is still a result.
